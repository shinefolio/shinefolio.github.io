FLOAT

Summer 2024
Solo Project/Designer
Genre: Simulation

Synopsis: 
Float is a 3D first-person floating simulator. You find yourself adrift in a surreal "liminal space". There are no monsters here, but the game might induce psychological fears associated with empty spaces, getting lost and nostalgia. Ever wanted to steer one of those bright pink flamingo floats? Well, now you can!

​An immersive experience inspired by the Poolrooms.

a liminal space experience

Liminal spaces are derived from an internet aesthetic that emerged on web forums in 2019, featuring places with a transitional or dreamlike quality. One of the best examples of the liminal space aesthetic is the internet phenomenon known as the Backrooms, said to be accessible only by “noclipping” through reality. Those who arrive find themselves in an unsettling maze of nondescript rooms, illuminated by sickly fluorescent lights that constantly flicker and hum.

This was a solo project to design a kinaesthetic experience based on the Poolrooms, a subcategory of this aesthetic that features a similarly undulating, otherworldly liminal space.
Credits / jaredpike

emotion by design
True to the concept’s aesthetic origins, Float should feel surreal and uneasily tranquil. While a common interpretation of the Poolrooms setting involves the player wading through the water, Float centres its core mechanic on steering a large flamingo floatie across the surface of the water and interacting with several physics-based objects drifting atop it.

The use of floating as a core experience disrupts the player’s usual sense of gravity and movement, distorting sensory perception and creating soft, fluid movement. This deepens the feeling of being in an otherworldly space, further detaching the player from the groundedness of reality.

the challenge

The goal of this project was to create a deeply immersive environment that captured the feel of a liminal space, with particular attention given to polishing the game's feel and overall experience. Cross-platform compatibility was also explored, with controller support providing additional tactile feedback during gameplay.

HIGH LEVEL GOALS

​1. Design an immersive, highly atmospheric environment that feels surreal and otherworldly
2. Ensure that player actions are accompanied by appropriate visual, auditory, and tactile feedback
3. Utilise technical tools such as shaders and post-processing effects to enhance the realism of the experience

MY ROLE

Float started development in Unity during the summer of 2024, concluding after about a month. As it was a solo project, I was responsible for design, programming and sourcing third party assets for sound and art. Supplementary programming assistance was obtained.

project kickoff

Research was conducted on the concept of liminal spaces and the common traits associated with them, including existing games that explore similar concepts. Liminal spaces often feature dim lighting, the hum of fluorescent lights, and environments that feel as though they were once busy but are now empty and abandoned. For the Poolrooms, this aesthetic is replicated with an environment that features endless rows of pristine white ceramic tiles, uniformly arranged, with clear, lukewarm pool water reflecting the blue-green hue of the tiles.

Additionally, I researched techniques for replicating realistic water shaders, drawing inspiration from liminal space videos on YouTube, particularly in terms of both art and sound design.
Credits / Midwich Music

game feel

As a project focused on kinaesthetic experience, I implemented a variety of "juicy" effects to enhance tactile feedback. These included particle effects simulating water splashes when the floats collide with objects in the pool. Camera shake and a slight Dutch tilt were also implemented for the player upon wall collisions.

​To complement this, the camera shake effect was combined with Unity’s Input System PlayerController to trigger controller rumble when using a game controller. The intensity of the rumble dynamically varies based on the size of the object the float collides with. Finally, post-processing effects were added to the game to create a motion blur effect, along with lighting tweaks for that final polishing touch.

SIMPLE PHYSICS-BASED ACTION
The flamingo floatie that the player rides features suspension, allowing it to bounce realistically against the surface of the water. To increase immersion for the experience and add an element of playfulness, I added smaller floaties that also incorporated similar physics-based action. They bob across the surface of the water, leaving ripples and drifting away when bumping against hard surfaces like walls and other floaties.

sound design

Distinct interaction feedback sounds were added for the objects in the pool, using custom foley I created with real pool floats and splashing water recorded at an actual pool. The sound effects and background music then underwent heavy post-processing, including reverb, echo, and the intentional muffling of certain elements to enhance the game's low-fidelity aesthetic. Finally, the processed layers were combined with ambient water dripping sounds and the iconic hum and buzz of fluorescent lights to create a surreal and immersive sound environment.

snorricam shot - film technique

The Snorricam is a body-mounted camera rig that locks the camera's perspective to the actor, capturing immersive, emotionally charged point-of-view (POV) shots. Float employs a Snorricam cinematographic technique to enhance immersion. Instead of locking onto the player’s POV, the camera locks onto the POV of the object directly in front of the player, in this case, the flamingo float.

​Integrating this cinematographic technique into Float deepens the player's sense of immersion, creating a realistic yet disorienting environment that supports the overall feel of a liminal space.

retrospective

Float required careful attention to game feel, environmental design, and sound design to accurately replicate the aesthetic of liminal spaces and support the relatively simple core mechanic of steering a large flamingo float across the water.

Several key elements were pivotal to the kinaesthetic experience. First, the flamingo float’s movement needed to feel inherently satisfying. This meant even small settings such as the weight of the object and the level of friction as it glided across the water had to be carefully adjusted and extensively tested. Second, the pool water had to look convincing, given the game's setting in the Poolrooms. This challenge marked my first deep dive into shader work. By following a detailed tutorial, I was able to replicate the appearance of realistic water, adding a layer of authenticity to the experience.

Finally, the use of post-processing effects, combined with layered sound design, helped to tie everything together. The echoes and subtle reverb enhanced the eerie, dimly lit atmosphere typical of liminal spaces, immersing players in an environment that felt empty yet alive.
