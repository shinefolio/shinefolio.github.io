INDIE WEB FEATURE

Spring 2026
Writer/Designer
Genre: Cultural Journalism

Synopsis: 
An article on the rise and fall of the Indie Web movement, a cultural phenomenon defined by legible human authorship and the design language of the old web. It examines the systemic forces behind its decline, what authorship means in a world where it can no longer be assumed, and why its subsequent revival may matter more now than ever.

SUBSTACK

further reading

The above is an article on the rise, fall, and unexpected comeback of the Indie Web across four sections.

Written in a nested revelation structure, the article opens on a meditation on internet archival, spotlights a GeoCities profile, covers internet phenomena like Dead Internet Theory, then zooms back in on a profile building on its spiritual successor two decades later. Each section is a layer that reveals the next.

It follows an emotional arc of loss, mourning, threat, and unexpected hope, inviting the reader to grieve before celebrating, which underscores the weight of the Indie Web's cultural impact. The chain of events is causally coherent, moving from the impact of the hand-coded web, to its shutdown, through social media homogenisation and bot proliferation, and finally landing on the NeoCities resistance.
​
​Comparative literary metaphors are woven throughout. The archive is positioned as a time machine that requires human intention to populate and exists only to preserve that same intention. GeoCities authors are rendered as ghosts, with their disappearance from the modern web used as a payoff that ties back to the opening
