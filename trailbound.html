TRAILBOUND

Fall 2025-Spring 2026
Game Design Intern/Narrative/Writer
Genre: Cozy Life Simulation/Multiplayer

Synopsis: 
Trailbound is a cozy life sim that turns your Discord server into a whimsical adventure. The successor to the widely popular Tatsu.gg, present in 1.4M+ servers, it represents the next step in community engagement and growth. Interact with community members to collect 800+ rewards like packs, furniture, and pets to decorate your home, craft and salvage materials, play minigames like fishing, and create a third space within your community.

PROJECT LINKS

trailbound

a community building cozy life-sim

Many Discord communities exhibit a well-documented pattern of engagement decay, a gradual erosion of active participation that when left unaddressed, leads to community decline. The causes compound when the number of active participants shrinks, creating a lack of interaction that feels worth the effort or social risk. Yet for the majority of members who stay, a pool of latent interest waits for a reason to activate.

I was part of a project to design a game that aims to leverage this gap by giving community members a reason and incentive to participate, driving engagement and retention through a gamified layer on Discord.

the challenge

The goal of this project was to deeply integrate community-building mechanics into the game, bringing the community's fantasies and experiences to life. Additionally, Trailbound needed to be versatile enough to adapt to the DNA of any community it was in, allowing players to actively shape the server’s legacy and become part of its ongoing story.


HIGH LEVEL GOALS

1. Plan and execute live-ops mechanics and events depending on seasonal requirements
2. Improve clarity of onboarding process and simplicity of game mechanics to increase D1 retention​
3. Integrate game mechanics with Discord’s native constructs to provide tangible, server-native ways for communities to express and reinforce their identity and culture

MY ROLE

I worked with the Game Design Team to design engagement and retention features for Trailbound. My responsibilities included planning live-ops events, writing game design documents  (GDDs) , balancing game mechanics through systems design, and writing narrative flavor and lore that established a framework for future content. I also experimented with agentic systems on Claude to accelerate game design production and shipping cadence.

​Additionally, I supported the marketing team with copywriting and editing of feature guides, dev blogs, and newsletters as part of live-ops communication with the player base.

first principles thinking

When designing Trailbound, I started with first-principles thinking by breaking down the problem (How can we build Discord Communities?) down into its fundamental truths and shaping the solution from there. I started by identifying the core human drivers that motivate interaction, just as individuality and progression, followed by identifying Discord’s native constructs.

Discord’s native constructs includes social components as roles, profiles, etc. I followed this by defining which were essential for the minimum viable product (MVP) versus auxiliary features (DLC) that could be added later to enable a faster shipping cadence.

live ops events

As part of the game design team, I contributed to the conceptualisation and development of seasonal live-ops events, for example the Winter (Rimenisce Festival) and Lunar New Year (Fortune Festival), designing feature loops and mechanics that aligned with each season's themes.

​These events were developed with key live ops success metrics in mind, spanning retention and engagement indicators such as D30 Retention, DAU/MAU Ratio, Daily Session Length, Session Frequency, and New Feature Adoption.
EVENT STRUCTURE
Each event was built around a structured weekly content rotation, with seasonal content released in stages across the event window to maintain momentum throughout the live ops cycle. These rotations also served as feature reveal moments, introducing new mechanics through event-exclusive content. For instance, during Lunar New Year (Fortune Festival), seasonal-exclusive fish for the Year of the Horse such as Seahorses were introduced to onboard and entice players to try the newly launched fishing minigame.

Events were designed with dual earn pathways in mind, allowing players to obtain seasonal rewards like exclusive pets (e.g., the Fortune Pet for Lunar New Year through in-game quests or paid bundles. This was complemented by rate-up mechanics for our gacha systems, designed to re-engage players and incentivise pack openings during the festive period.

Meanwhile, limited-time event materials (e.g., Snowdrop for the Winter set) and furniture gave players a sustained progression path toward crafting a full seasonal furniture set, adding more opportunities for player expression and customisation. 

Occasionally, UGC events were also introduced alongside seasonal campaigns, motivating community participation and encouraging the accumulation of items from the new seasonal furniture set. These friendly competitions were hosted through Discord to engage the player base, with the winners of the competition receiving special rewards.
LIVE-OPS NARRATIVE DIRECTION
Narrative and lore played a central role in grounding each event within the game world. I contributed by providing thematic flavour for each seasonal event, creating moodboards for characters, outfits, and furniture styles, ensuring thematic cohesion across various live ops touchpoints from in-game mechanics to external communications.

meta-dynamics in design

SOCIAL CURVE DESIGN
A core consideration when designing game mechanics for Trailbound was social curve design, specifically how to design artificial inconveniences that created organic reasons for players to collaborate. Certain game mechanics were shaped around social challenges that necessitated collective action, such as a community crafting goal where players contributed toward a visible, server-wide milestone.

​Intrinsic and extrinsic motivation, particularly heroic mechanics, gave players a way to contribute toward a shared outcome, elevating their social standing and empowering members to feel like they matter when milestones were hit.
GAME NARRATIVE DIRECTION
Trailbound necessitated a shift in the traditional way of creating a game narrative. As the game needed to be capable of being shaped and integrated into the DNA of the community, the narrative flair had to exist as a thin layer. Rather than anchoring players to a fixed internal lore, the narrative was designed as a flexible structure that could integrate across different communities.

This was rooted in the idea that emotional stakes drive relevance. A player may not care about a fictional cat, but if that cat is based on a beloved server mascot, the stakes become personal and relatable. By keeping the narrative framing deliberately neutral, communities are given the space to shape it around their own identity and culture, allowing the game to transcend age groups and server cultures, meeting players where their existing interest lives.

monetisation

As Trailbound is a live service model (platform monetisation), this meant designing a monetisation ecosystem built around ongoing engagement rather than a single transaction. The free-to-play model allowed us to observe player behaviour and gauge willingness to pay before going to market, using player engagement data to inform Long Term Value (LTV) considerations.

Effective monetisation either creates an artificial problem or addresses a genuine human need, then offers a solution players are willing to pay for. This informed the seasonal monetisation cycle, where exclusive time-limited content such as Legendary Pets created a recurring sense of desirability tied to social standing during each seasonal live-ops cycle. Gacha and pity systems were also considered as a monetisation layer, designed to satisfy the psychological appeal of chance-based rewards.

Across all monetisation considerations, the underlying purpose remained consistent: to give players a compelling reason to return and reactivate within the game.

The key design consideration was the problem statement: Does it help build the community? This shaped how mechanics were designed around collective action and social standing, how the narrative was kept neutral for communities to make it their own, and how live-ops was approached as a tool for retention and engagement.

Alongside this was the consideration of increasing the surface area of the product, allowing players to engage with the game through both Discord activity and the Discord bot, creating more points of entry beyond the core loop. Working within a fast-paced live ops environment meant making deliberate decisions between designing for quick wins (high impact and low effort) versus investing in meaningful strategic goals that kept the broader vision of the product in mind.
