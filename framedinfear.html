FRAMED IN FEAR

Spring 2025
Designer/Level Designer
Genre: Horror

Synopsis: 
Framed In Fear is a 3D first-person horror game. You are trapped in a dark, shifting and abandoned house. Uncover torn photographs of the house's past owners and piece together a haunting story. Survival means outmaneuvering your friends. But beware! The house's grip tightens, and in the end, no one truly escapes...

a psychological horror game of eldritch madness

Haunted houses have been a staple in horror games since time immemorial, a reflection of emotionally-charged environments coexisting with the supernatural. Combined with elements of eldritch horror, Framed in Fear expands on this setting, integrating puzzles with hidden object mechanics.

I was part of a project to design a game focusing on a twist, a catharsis, and a sense of closure, using the fundamental human emotion of fear.

Framed in Fear explores themes of psychological horror, betrayal, and the illusion of control.

The game plays with the Prisoner’s Dilemma, forcing players to choose between cooperation and self-preservation, while the house itself serves as a metaphor for inescapable fate and the cyclical nature of suffering.

the challenge

The goal of this project was to create a narrative and strong emotional perspective through the game’s concept, mechanics, and environments. Including at least one multiplayer/social mechanic to foster engagement or create potential for user-generated or user participative content.

HIGH LEVEL GOALS

1. Create an immersive, haunted house environment that induces a sense of dread and paranoia
2. Design a level that supports the core hidden object + puzzle mechanic
3. Use narrative tools such as cutscenes to convey the storyline effectively

MY ROLE

Framed in Fear began development in Unity in the spring of 2025, concluding after about a month and a half. I took on the role of Level Designer and contributed to various aspects of narrative design. As the Level Designer, I crafted a detailed level floorplan and mapped out the placement of key interactive elements within the environment. Additionally, I experimented with comic-style narrative techniques to convey the game's backstory without using words.

project kickoff

At the beginning of the project, we conducted research on psychological studies related to creating a sense of false hope and entrapment, integrating it into the game’s mechanics. Players initially believe they are restoring the photographs in a puzzle in order to escape, only to realise by the end of the game that their efforts are futile, which intensifies their sense of helplessness.

​Additionally, we decided to create a circular narrative through the use of cutscenes, where we show the final player “escaping,” only to become trapped in a photograph, becoming part of the house’s endless cycle. The closure is inescapable and final, leaving players with a sense of dread and the realisation that they were never truly free.

ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING
The game manipulates the environment to create an intentionally unreliable horror experience. Environmental storytelling plays a central role in the narrative, evoking a sense of dread through subtle visual cues that flicker just at the edge of the player’s peripheral vision. One example is the picture frames on the walls, which flicker after a set amount of time to reveal disturbing images hidden beneath.

To deepen the environmental storytelling, we embedded clues about the original owner’s career as a photographer throughout the house. This is evident with the presence of a darkroom used for photo development in the house and a conspiracy board in the living room, where photographs are hastily pinned to a corkboard.

The overall layout of the house is inspired by traditional gothic architecture, featuring gaudy interiors and intricately patterned wallpapers. The foyer serves as the central nexus of the house, acting as the game's start point and the location of the core puzzle, which highlights the room’s importance.

Framed in Fear has a mechanic where players reorder the frames of a story told as a series of wordless images.

A man inadvertently takes a photograph of an eldritch entity and his young son at a remote location, resulting in the entity following him home. He tries to conduct research about this entity, realising that it takes the life of the subject it is pictured in the photo with. In a final, desperate effort, he builds a shrine for the entity in his foyer, hoping to appease it. However, it takes the life of his son as he falls into a manic state, abandoning the house to ruin.

post-processing

Post-processing was used to create dim lighting, evoking a sense of dread and paranoia throughout the haunted house. Key environmental lights were strategically placed to highlight important doorways and guide player navigation throughout the level. Periodically, lightning flashes outside, accompanied by cracks of thunder. A lens bloom effect intensifies these flashes, momentarily blinding the player to heighten tension. Additional post-processing effects such as vignette and film grain contribute to a retro aesthetic, while split toning adds a distinctive colour tint to enhance the overall mood.

retrospective

From designing shifting environments that distort reality to crafting a narrative that blurs the line between trust and betrayal, Framed in Fear challenged us to create an immersive, atmospheric world with strong psychological horror elements. By utilising environmental storytelling, it allowed us to craft an experience that keeps players on edge.

​There is no lurking entity in the house, yet in the darkness, it always feels like something is watching. Every darkened corridor becomes a fertile ground for the imagination, and this fear of the unknown serves as our greatest tool to evoke paranoia.
